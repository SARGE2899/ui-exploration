01 — Buttons & Clicks

Primary Action

Click here to trigger the main action on a page

The primary button is the most important action. There should only be one per screen. Used for "Submit", "Sign Up", "Buy Now" — things you want the user to do most.

Try it

The pill shape and blue fill signal iOS's most important action — instantly familiar.

Secondary Action

Click here to take a less important action

Secondary buttons sit alongside primary ones. Used for "Cancel", "Go Back", "Save Draft" — actions that are valid but not the main goal.

Try it

Paired together, hierarchy is instantly clear — Save is more important than Cancel.

Destructive Action

Click here to do something that cannot be undone

Danger buttons warn users before irreversible actions like "Delete Account", "Remove File", or "Clear All". iOS uses red as a universal warning signal.

Try it

Real apps show an Action Sheet confirmation before executing — e.g. "Are you sure?"

Icon Button

Click here to perform a quick action with just an icon

Icon buttons save space in toolbars and cards. Used for Like, Share, Bookmark, Close. They rely on universally understood symbols — always add a tooltip for clarity.

Try them

Circular shape is the iOS standard for icon-only buttons in toolbars and navigation.

02 — Forms & Inputs

Text Input

Click here to type a single line of text

The most common form element. Used for names, emails, search, passwords. Notice the blue ring when focused — the iOS focus indicator tells the user "I am active here."

Try it — tap inside and type

Hint text below the input gives extra guidance.

Textarea

Click here to type multiple lines of text

When you need more than one line — like a message, bio, or notes. Users can drag the bottom-right corner to resize. Real apps often show a character count.

Try it — type and resize

Dropdown Select

Click here to choose one option from a list

Dropdowns keep long lists tidy. Used for country, category, month. Best when there are 5–15 choices. On iOS, this opens a native picker wheel.

Try it

Checkbox

Click here to turn an option on or off

Checkboxes are for yes/no choices or selecting multiple items from a list. Key difference from radio buttons: you can check many at once.

Try checking multiple

Radio Button

Click here to select exactly one option from a group

Radio buttons enforce a single choice. Used for plan selection, payment method. Selecting one automatically deselects the others — unlike checkboxes.

Toggle Switch

Click here to instantly switch a setting on or off

iOS toggles are the gold standard — they apply changes immediately, no Save needed. Used in Settings for Dark Mode, Notifications, Do Not Disturb.

03 — Images & Media

Hero Image

The large image at the top that sets the mood of a page

Hero images are the first visual a user sees. They establish brand tone, context, and emotion. Often paired with a headline and a CTA button on top.

The small circular image that represents a person

Avatars appear in comments, profiles, nav bars, and messages. When no photo is available, initials are shown. Size communicates importance — larger = more prominent.

04 — Navigation & Links

Top Navigation Bar

The bar at the top — tap links to move between pages

The navbar stays visible on every page. It orients the user ("where am I?") and lets them jump anywhere. The active link is blue — iOS's standard for the current selection.

Breadcrumb

Breadcrumbs show where you are in a multi-level structure. Common on App Store and Settings. They answer: "How did I get here, and how do I go back?"

Segmented Control

Click a segment to switch between views without leaving the page

iOS's Segmented Control swaps content instantly in the same space. It's the native alternative to tabs — used in Maps (Map/Transit/Satellite), Photos, and Calendar views.

Pagination

Click here to move between pages of a long list

Pagination breaks large datasets into manageable pages. The alternative is infinite scroll — both have trade-offs. iOS often uses infinite scroll; web apps tend to use pagination.

Sidebar Navigation

Click a sidebar item to go to that section of the app

Sidebar navs are used in iPad and macOS apps — Finder, Mail, Notes, Xcode. They keep all destinations visible so users can jump around quickly without hunting for links.
